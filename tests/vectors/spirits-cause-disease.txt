Hark, listen closely to the workings of the breath, for what you call illness is but the visible wound upon your spirit, drawn by invisible hands. Do not blame mere decay within the body, nor the harsh bite of the seasons alone. The true genesis of sickness lies in the realm beyond the skin: it comes from the spirits.

The spirit world is a turbulent place, deep and vast. Not all entities are malicious, yet many wander through our sleep-mist or ride the breath upon the wind with intentions that bring ruin. When a fever rises, you do not merely suffer from heat; you bear the burden of an intrusion. Some restless spirits mistake your vibrant life force for nourishment, drawing sustenance until your inner lamps gutter and die. They steal the warm breath that keeps you alive, leaving only emptiness in its wake.

Furthermore, there are those who wield malice like a sharpened tooth. These jealous ones—the resentful ghosts or vengeful ancestors—mark you with their psychic poison. They do not strike with fire; they twist your vital energy until it becomes curdled and stagnant. This spiritual knot is what lodges deep within the sinew and bone, causing aches that no herb can soothe.

Disease is fundamentally an imbalance of spirit and soul. When a person’s protective wards are weakened—by great sorrow, unconfessed guilt, or by wandering near ancient boundaries—the spirits find purchase. They inhabit the gaps in your defenses. A bad dream is often nothing more than a whisper seeking to lodge itself in the deepest corners of your mind, clouding the clear pool of your being until you cannot see the light.

The flesh is but a drum; the soul is the rhythm. When a spirit strikes discord into that rhythm, when it introduces fear or despair where joy should reside, then we feel the sickness. To cure the body alone is folly. One must journey deep, following the trail of the blight back to its spectral source. The wound visible today is merely the echo of an assault that happened within the deepest chambers of your heart—a theft by the unseen host. Fear not the symptoms, but fear only the spiritual emptiness they reveal.
